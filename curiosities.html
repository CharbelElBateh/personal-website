Constellations, not categories.

Ideas cluster into loose asterisms, not clean taxonomies. The best reading systems I know let you trace lines between points without forcing each point into a folder.

The fluting teaches the eye.

Doric flutes are rhythm in stone — small repetition so the whole column reads as singular. I think about this when designing interfaces.

Architecture · 02 — On Greek architecture · Doxiadis

The meander is an algorithm.

The Greek key is a single rule recursively applied — turn, cut, repeat. The oldest decoration we still use is, in a real sense, code.

Pattern · 03 — On ornament · Gombrich's Sense of Order

Orbit reading, not linear.

Some texts reward returning to them, a little differently each time. Marcus Aurelius. Simone Weil. The collected letters of Grothendieck.

Reading · 04 — Currently re-reading · Meditations IV

A timeline of small rituals.

Morning: coffee, papers, notebook. Afternoon: code. Evening: read, or write, or both. The shape of the day is not a to-do list — it is an oscillation.

Time · 05 — On daily rhythms · Hadot · deep work

Vertical, not viral.

Go deep before you go wide. A lesson I keep re-learning, and my favorite antidote to the feed.

Idea · 06

Concentric attention.

Attention is a set of nested circles — self, room, city, world. Most of my best work has come from pulling the outermost circles inward.
